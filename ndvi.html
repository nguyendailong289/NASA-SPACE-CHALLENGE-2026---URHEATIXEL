PART 04 · DRIVER (2)

Vegetation is shrinking where the city expands

Annual NDVI is built from 24 MODIS MOD13A2 images (1 km, 16-day composites) per year. Values are min–max normalized to 0–1 within each year, then split into 5 classes in steps of 0.2. Overlaying the 2017 and 2025 class layers shows which pixels lost, kept or gained vegetation class. Losing vegetation removes the surface's natural cooling and helps the high-heat zone spread.

    CONCLUSION

    The high-NDVI zone (High + Very High) shrank from 52.7% to 41.6% of the area. The area losing NDVI class (676.5 km²) is about 2.1 times the area gaining class (324.7 km²). Pixels that lost class have an average built-up expansion share of 10.6%, higher than unchanged pixels (8.6%) and pixels that gained class (7.6%), and lie closer to the center (24.8 km vs 34.3 km). Greenery is being lost exactly where the city is spreading. Part 05 overlays temperature and vegetation to find where to intervene first.
